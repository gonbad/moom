# Editorial, visual and artifact checks

Map every required claim, action and creator correction to the finished post. Verify
quotations, dates, names, links and attribution against primary sources; separate the
author's experience from inference. Read the complete page without oral narration:
it must supply its own context, explanations and transitions. Keep editorial audit
notes out of reader content unless specifically requested.

Review a content sample and its layout together, then inspect the complete output.
Default to vertical reading on desktop and mobile. Exercise navigation, copy controls,
persisted progress, reset, printing and any intentional downloads where provided.
Test narrow RTL layouts and long mixed-script strings. Interact with widgets at normal,
boundary and error states, comparing expected results against independent references
when calculations matter. An HTML string/ID check does not execute a widget or prove
mathematical correctness. Record browser behavior, console/network errors and visual
observations separately from static checks and hashes. Keep reproducible dependencies
and commands under `projects/ID/checks/` and evidence in the owning version dossier.

Check the exact web release inventory and its authored index, links and private/public
boundary. Ordinary blog posts do not require an offline archive. If a downloadable
artifact was explicitly requested, verify that exact deliverable using its agreed
acceptance criteria. Intentional editorial materials retain their checksum pins.
Verify public origin and supported aliases on deployment. A clean console, hash match
or successful deployment is not human editorial acceptance. Preserve actual earlier
acceptance and delivery evidence; do not invent new approval after a shared-policy fix.
