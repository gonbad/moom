# Production and revision

A sequence follows a dramatic goal. Within it, a scene holds a continuous time and place. A shot is one camera view. A cut changes view. Framing selects what fits inside it. A pan turns the camera from one subject toward another. Use this vocabulary in production notes. Ask the owner “What should we see when she opens the door?”

Maintain a shot list in the Project with stable IDs independent of order, sequence and scene membership, frame, action, camera, entrance/exit states, duration, narration take, cue offsets and cut. A cue ties a sound or action to an observable event. Keep a separate asset list mapping IDs to approved sources, provenance, states, transitions and shot uses. A state may describe clothing, expression or pose. Add variants without replacing earlier states. Choose a starting state for each shot and animate a transition only when the audience sees it happen. Separate a prop or foreground layer when it moves, occludes something or recurs. For generated backgrounds save the exact image and its prompt.

Keep review notes in the current numbered Markdown dossier. Link playable previews. Explicitly distinguish technical evidence, creative decisions and human acceptance of the named revision. For self-directed production record who actually made creative decisions. Never sign a participant's review. Use the review constitution for submissions. Offer a speaking part or manual recording import before assuming a paid voice service. Retain separate takes. Check speech over effects, breaths, silence and pronunciation in context.

Planned changes use the permanent iterate procedure. For a new costume add a state, show one shot, update its specified appearances and inspect adjacent cuts. For a rewritten line return to the prompt for the affected performance (speaking turn, jointly recorded scene or continuously narrated sequence), retain the previously used revision, record the new take and audition it in the affected passage first, then retime its cues, review the boundary, rebuild its sequence and assemble the master. For a concrete defect use issue, reproduce it, repair its source, then check affected uses. Cache valid shots and sequence renders with input identities. Report reusable improvements in a method note. Preserve the previous delivery until the new MP4 passes. A longer film adds sequences under the same workflow. Keep the board, rough cut and running times for the whole story so that local edits can be judged in context.
